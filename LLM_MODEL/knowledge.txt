ABSTRACT 
Food wastage is a critical social issue that exists alongside widespread food insecurity. Large 
quantities of surplus food from households, events, and organizations are often discarded due to 
the absence of an efficient and reliable redistribution system. To address this problem, this 
project presents DWJD (Don’t Waste, Just Donate), a web-based donation and delivery 
management platform designed to connect donors, delivery riders, and recipients through a 
structured and secure workflow. 
The system enables users to donate food and essential items by submitting donation details 
through an intuitive interface. Delivery riders can view available pickup requests, navigate to 
donor locations, verify donations using OTP-based authentication, and complete deliveries with 
real-time status tracking. An OTP verification mechanism ensures that donations are handed over 
securely and prevents unauthorized pickups or deliveries. 
The platform also includes an Admin module that provides overall system insights such as user 
statistics, delivery performance, rejected pickups, and active promotional advertisements. This 
module allows administrators to monitor system activity and manage promotional content 
displayed to users, supporting scalability and future monetization. 
The application is developed using React and Vite for the frontend, Node.js and Express.js for 
the backend, and MongoDB for data storage. Secure authentication is implemented using JWT, 
and email-based OTP verification is used to maintain trust and integrity throughout the donation 
lifecycle. The system is deployed on cloud platforms to ensure accessibility, performance, and 
reliability. 
Overall, DWJD provides a practical, scalable, and technology-driven solution to reduce food 
wastage while promoting community-driven social responsibility. 
INTRODUCTION 
Food wastage has become a serious global challenge despite the growing number 
of people facing food insecurity. Large quantities of edible food are discarded daily 
from households, restaurants, events, and organizations due to the lack of a proper 
redistribution mechanism. At the same time, NGOs, shelters, and individuals 
struggle to access timely food resources. This imbalance highlights the need for a 
structured and technology-driven solution that can bridge the gap between surplus 
food providers and those in need. 
With the rapid advancement of web technologies, digital platforms can play a 
crucial role in addressing real-world social problems. However, many existing 
donation systems lack transparency, real-time tracking, verification mechanisms, 
and proper role-based management. Issues such as unauthorized pickups, lack of 
accountability, and poor coordination between donors and delivery personnel 
reduce trust and efficiency in traditional donation workflows. 
To overcome these challenges, DWJD (Don’t Waste, Just Donate) is developed 
as a full-stack web application that facilitates secure, transparent, and organized 
donation and delivery management. The platform connects three primary 
stakeholders: Users (donors), Riders (delivery personnel), and Administrators. 
Each role is provided with a dedicated interface and controlled access to ensure 
smooth operation and accountability. 
The system introduces OTP-based verification for donation pickup and delivery, 
ensuring that items are handed over only to authorized riders. Role-based 
dashboards allow users to track donation status, riders to manage pickups and 
deliveries, and administrators to monitor system performance through aggregated 
statistics and promotional management. By leveraging modern web technologies 
and cloud deployment, the platform ensures scalability, reliability, and 
accessibility across devices. 
DWJD aims to reduce food wastage, improve donation transparency, and 
encourage community participation through a secure and user-friendly digital 
solution 
PROBLEM STATEMENT 
Food wastage remains a persistent issue in society, even though many individuals and 
organizations are willing to donate surplus food, clothes, and essential items. The absence of a 
centralized, reliable, and secure platform to manage donations results in inefficient distribution, 
lack of accountability, and misuse of donated resources. Existing informal donation methods rely 
heavily on manual coordination, which often leads to delayed pickups, incorrect handovers, and 
limited tracking of donated items. 
One of the major challenges in donation systems is the lack of verification and role-based 
control. Without proper authentication mechanisms, it becomes difficult to ensure that donated 
items are collected by the correct delivery personnel and delivered to the intended destination. 
This can lead to unauthorized pickups, lost donations, or disputes between donors and 
volunteers. Additionally, donors usually have no visibility into whether their donations are 
successfully delivered, reducing trust in the system. 
Another significant issue is the absence of real-time monitoring and administrative oversight. 
Without an admin-level view, it is hard to analyze overall system performance, such as the 
number of active users, riders, completed deliveries, rejected pickups, and ongoing promotions. 
This limits scalability and prevents organizations from improving donation workflows based on 
data-driven insights. 
Furthermore, current systems rarely support secure communication and validation 
mechanisms like OTP verification, which are essential for ensuring delivery authenticity. The 
lack of structured dashboards for different stakeholders—users, riders, and administrators—
creates confusion and reduces operational efficiency. 
Therefore, there is a strong need for a secure, role-based, and scalable donation management 
platform that ensures transparency, verification, and efficient coordination between donors, 
delivery personnel, and administrators, while providing real-time tracking and analytical insights 
to reduce food wastage and improve community support systems.

o Donation submission 
o OTP verification 
 Displays real-time donation status updates 
 Communicates with the backend via RESTful APIs using HTTP requests 
2. Application Layer (Backend / Server) 
The application layer handles business logic and acts as a bridge between the frontend and 
database. 
Technologies Used: 
 Node.js 
 Express.js 
Responsibilities: 
 User authentication and authorization

SECURITY IMPLEMENTATION 
Security is a critical aspect of the DWJD (Don’t Waste, Just Donate) platform, as it handles user 
authentication, personal data, and donation transactions. The system implements multiple layers 
of security to protect data confidentiality, integrity, and authorized access. 
1. Authentication Security 
1.1 Password Encryption 
 User, Rider, and Admin passwords are never stored in plain text. 
 Passwords are encrypted using bcrypt hashing algorithm before storing in the database. 
 During login, the entered password is verified using secure hash comparison. 
Benefit: 
Prevents password leakage even if the database is compromised. 
1.2 OTP-Based Verification 
 OTP (One-Time Password) is generated during: 
o User registration 
o Donation confirmation 
 OTP is: 
o 6-digit random number 
o Time-bound (expires after 10 minutes) 
 OTP is stored temporarily and removed after successful verification. 
Benefit: 
Ensures only legitimate users and donors can perform sensitive actions. 
2. Authorization & Role-Based Access Control 
The system follows Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) with three roles: 
Role 
Access Level 
User Donate items, view requests 
Rider Pick, verify, and deliver donations 
Admin Monitor system statistics and manage promotions 
 Routes are protected based on the user role. 
 Admin access is restricted strictly to admin accounts. 
 Riders cannot access user-only or admin-only endpoints. 
Benefit: 
Prevents unauthorized access and privilege escalation. 
3. Token-Based Security (JWT) 
 On successful login, a JSON Web Token (JWT) is issued. 
 Token contains: 
o User ID 
o Role (user / rider / admin) 
 Token has an expiry time (7 days). 
Benefit: 
Provides stateless and secure session management. 
4. Data Validation & Input Sanitization 
 All incoming API requests are validated: 
o Required fields are checked 
o Invalid or missing data is rejected 
 Server-side validation prevents: 
o Invalid input 
o Malformed requests 
Benefit: 
Protects against injection attacks and malformed data submission. 
5. OTP & Transaction Integrity 
 Donation pickup requires OTP verification from donor. 
 Without OTP verification: 
o Delivery cannot be marked as completed 
 Prevents riders from falsely marking deliveries. 
Benefit: 
Ensures trust and transparency between users and riders. 
6. Secure Database Design 
 Sensitive fields such as: 
o Passwords 
o OTP values 
 Are either encrypted or removed after use. 
 MongoDB schema enforces: 
o Field types 
o Required constraints 
Benefit: 
Maintains data consistency and reduces exposure of sensitive data. 
7. CORS & API Protection 
 CORS is enabled to allow only trusted frontend origins. 
 Prevents unauthorized cross-origin requests. 
Benefit: 
Protects backend APIs from malicious external access. 
8. Error Handling & Logging 
 Generic error messages are returned to clients. 
 Detailed error logs are maintained on the server. 
 Prevents exposure of internal system details.

1. Deployment Architecture Overview 
The application is deployed in a three-tier architecture: 
1. Frontend Layer – User Interface 
2. Backend Layer – Business Logic & APIs 
3. Database Layer – Persistent Data Storage 
Each layer is deployed on a dedicated platform to improve performance and maintainability. 
2. Frontend Deployment 
Platform Used: 
Vercel 
Details: 
 The frontend is developed using React + Vite. 
 The production build is generated using Vite’s optimized build process. 
 The build output is deployed on Vercel, which provides: 
o Global CDN 
o Fast page loads 
o Automatic HTTPS 
 Environment variables are configured to connect with the backend API hosted on Render. 
Benefits: 
 Fast global delivery 
 Automatic redeployment on code updates 
 Secure and scalable hosting 
3. Backend Deployment 
Platform Used: 
Render 
Details: 
 Backend is built using Node.js and Express.js. 
 RESTful APIs handle authentication, donations, rider operations, admin management, 
and advertisements. 
 The backend is deployed as a web service on Render. 
 Environment variables include: 
o MongoDB connection string 
o JWT secret 
o Email service credentials 
 Health check endpoint is used to monitor service availability. 
Benefits: 
 Simple CI/CD pipeline 
 Automatic server restarts 
 Easy environment configuration 
4. Database Deployment 
Platform Used: 
MongoDB Atlas (Cloud Database) 
Details: 
 MongoDB Atlas provides a fully managed cloud database. 
 Collections include: 
o Users 
o Donations 
o Advertisements 
 Secure connection using MongoDB URI. 
 Database access is restricted using IP whitelisting and authentication credentials. 
Benefits: 
 High availability 
 Automatic backups 
 Scalable storage 
5. Environment Configuration 
Sensitive configuration values are stored using environment variables: 
 MONGO_URI 
 JWT_SECRET 
 RESEND_API_KEY 
 PORT 
This ensures: 
 Security of credentials 
 Separation of development and production environments

1. Code is developed and tested locally.

o Automatically deployed by Vercel on push.

 HTTPS is enforced on both frontend and backend.

 Frontend uses CDN-based delivery.

CHALLENGES FACED 
During the development of the DWJD (Don’t Waste, Just Donate) application, several technical 
and practical challenges were encountered. These challenges played a crucial role in improving 
system design, debugging skills, and real-world problem-solving abilities. 
1. OTP Delivery Delays and Failures 
Challenge 
 OTP emails were delivered instantly in localhost but were delayed or blocked in 
production (Render + Gmail). 
 Free email services imposed restrictions and spam filtering. 
Solution 
 Analyzed SMTP limitations and identified Gmail throttling. 
 Integrated Resend Email API for faster and reliable OTP delivery. 
 Implemented non-blocking OTP sending to avoid API timeouts. 
Learning: 
Production environments behave differently from local systems. 
2. Role-Based Authentication Complexity 
Challenge 
 Handling three roles (User, Rider, Admin) with different access levels. 
 Preventing unauthorized page access. 
 Admin login needed to work without exposing admin options publicly. 
Solution 
 Implemented role detection from database instead of frontend selection. 
 Restricted UI rendering based on user_type. 
 Secured admin dashboard access through backend validation. 
Learning: 
Authentication must be enforced on both frontend and backend. 
3. OTP-Based Pickup Verification Logic 
Challenge 
 Preventing riders from completing deliveries without OTP verification. 
 Handling edge cases where the rider leaves the page mid-process. 
Solution 
 Introduced OTP verification state before enabling delivery. 
 Ensured backend checks OTP verification before status changes. 
 Added recovery logic to resume incomplete pickups safely. 
Learning: 
State management is critical in real-time workflows. 
4. Navigation and Route Management Issues 
Challenge 
 Blank screens due to incorrect routing. 
 Nested routes not rendering expected components. 
 Admin routes conflicting with user routes. 
Solution 
 Reorganized routes using nested routing with Outlet. 
 Fixed incorrect paths (/overall → /afterlogin/overall). 
 Centralized route control inside Afterlogin.jsx. 
Learning: 
Proper route hierarchy prevents UI failures. 
5. MongoDB Schema and Data Consistency 
Challenge 
 Missing fields caused runtime errors. 
 Status mismatches between frontend and backend. 
Solution 
 Refined Mongoose schemas with defaults and enums. 
 Enforced validation at both schema and API levels. 
 Added timestamps for better tracking. 
Learning: 
A strong database schema reduces bugs significantly. 
6. Deployment Environment Differences 
Challenge 
 APIs working locally but failing in production. 
 Environment variables not loading correctly. 
Solution 
 Verified environment variables in Render dashboard. 
 Added health-check APIs. 
 Logged server behavior for production debugging. 
Learning: 
Deployment testing is as important as development testing. 
7. Advertisement Module Integration 
Challenge 
 Integrating promotions dynamically into user UI. 
 Ensuring only active advertisements are shown. 
Solution 
 Created a dedicated advertisements collection. 
 Implemented admin-only advertisement creation. 
 Filtered active promotions using backend APIs. 
Learning: 
Modular design simplifies feature expansion. 
8. Error Handling and User Experience 
Challenge 
 Raw server errors confusing users. 
 No feedback during failures. 
Solution 
 Added user-friendly error messages. 
 Implemented loading states and alerts. 
 Logged errors internally for debugging. 
Learning: 
Good UX includes clear error communication.

CONCLUSION 
The DWJD (Don’t Waste, Just Donate) platform successfully addresses the problem of food and

also introduces a sustainable operational model. The system is designed with future expansion in

efficiency with real-world problem solving. This project reflects strong backend logic, clean 
frontend design, and thoughtful system architecture, making it suitable for real-time deployment 
and further enhancement.
